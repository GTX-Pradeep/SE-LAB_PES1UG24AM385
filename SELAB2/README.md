# Community Solar Credit Allocation Manager (CSCAM)

## Lab 2 – Agile Sprint Planning and Execution

### Project Overview
The Community Solar Credit Allocation Manager (CSCAM) is designed to manage solar energy generation, credit allocation, peer-to-peer credit transfers, billing reconciliation, co-op management, and notifications.

### Objectives
- Break requirements into Epics and User Stories.
- Prioritize the product backlog.
- Estimate stories using Fibonacci Story Points.
- Plan and execute simulated sprints.
- Track work using Jira.
- Analyze sprint progress using the Burndown Chart.
- Reflect on sprint planning and execution.

### Epics
1. **Epic 1: Solar Generation Monitoring**
2. **Epic 2: Peer-to-Peer Credit Transfer**
3. **Epic 3: Billing Offset & Reconciliation**
4. **Epic 4: Co-op Management Dashboard**
5. **Epic 5: Notifications & Alerts**

### Sprint Workflow
The stories were tracked through the following Jira workflow:

**To Do → In Progress → In Review → Done**

Unfinished stories were carried forward to the next sprint.

### Estimation & Tracking
Fibonacci Story Points were used to estimate the effort required for each user story. Sprint progress and remaining work were analyzed using the Jira Burndown Chart.

### Reflection

**1. Did your estimations reflect the actual effort?**  
Yes, the story point estimations were reasonably close to the actual effort required. Some tasks took more effort than expected, but overall, the Fibonacci-based estimates helped us plan the sprint effectively.

**2. Was your backlog well-prioritized?**  
Yes, the backlog was well-prioritized based on the importance and dependencies of the requirements. Higher-priority and essential functionalities were considered first.

**3. How did your simulated sprint align with your plan?**  
The simulated sprint generally aligned with our plan. Stories were moved through To Do, In Progress, In Review, and Done. Some stories remained unfinished and were carried forward to the next sprint.

**4. What insights did the burndown chart give about your team's capacity?**  
The burndown chart showed how the remaining work changed throughout the sprint. It helped us understand the team's progress and capacity and showed that a significant portion of the planned work was completed.
